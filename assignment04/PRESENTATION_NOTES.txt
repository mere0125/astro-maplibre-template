The Temporary Forest
Assignment 4 - two-minute presentation
Mere Cui

My project, The Temporary Forest, asks what is hidden within the category
of vacant land. This website connects a citywide administrative inventory
with a close reading of fourteen East New York parcels.

The city map begins with 24,663 PLUTO records classified as vacant. Only
24,213 have usable boundaries, so I keep the record count separate from
the mapped count.

The surface folio opens with two lots: 492 Glenmore Avenue and 943 Dumont
Avenue. Both appear open in the 1996 and 2018 images. Both are classified
vacant in the 2026 record. Their reported areas are also similar. Yet
their 2022 vegetation responses are 92.3 and 0.3 percent.

I chose these contrasting examples to make the limits of the category
visible. They are not a controlled comparison, and two historical images
do not prove continuous vacancy.

Below them, fourteen actual parcel shapes form a common-scale image
folio. Each cutout uses a 300-foot square extent, with real 2022 pixels
inside the current boundary. Selecting a shape opens its location and
dated evidence on the map. A downloadable register keeps the underlying
values available for inspection.

The percentage measures valid pixels with NDVI above 0.20. It is a
spectral vegetation indicator, not forest area or habitat quality. Across
the two historical groups, median responses are almost identical: 63.1
and 63.6 percent. This pilot does not establish that earlier vacancy
produces more vegetation.

The folio uses sage paper colours and Times typography to encourage
careful observation. It helps the larger atlas identify contrasting cases
for fieldwork while showing where its evidence remains incomplete.

Presentation sequence

1. Start at New York City and point out the distinction between records
   and mapped features.
2. Open “the surfaces” and compare the two 2022 images, noting their same
   two-date history, current classification, and different responses.
3. Continue to the fourteen common-scale parcel cutouts. Select one to
   open its location and evidence on the map.
4. Compare dated images or use a filter in East New York. Explain the
   254-lot screening context and the fourteen deliberately selected sites.
5. End with the similar group medians and the need for field observation.
